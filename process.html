Design Process

In terms of the Information Architecture design, I divided the website into five main sections based on the assignment requirements: the About Me section on the homepage, and four subpages: Portfolio, Resume, Contact Information, and Process. Each page has a Header at the top that provides Global Navigation back to the homepage. The Navigation on the homepage links to each subpage, allowing users to quickly browse the content structure. When the mouse hovers over the Navigation, the font changes accordingly, providing clear interactive feedback. Each page also includes a footer with Doreen Lin Personal Website to maintain consistency throughout the website.

In addition to designing each subpage according to the required content and format, I also added Local Navigation and heading to indicate the topic of the current page, allowing users to clearly understand their current location within the website. Users can also use the Global Navigation to return to the homepage or navigate to other main pages at any time, creating a clear website navigation structure.

For the colour scheme, I chose warm brown tones as the theme colours to make viewers feel comfortable and welcomed while browsing my website. I also used an AI colour palette to select related colour combinations. When applying the colours from the palette, I arranged different shades according to the hierarchy of the structure. The Global Navigation uses the darkest colour, followed by the Local Navigation, while the main section uses the lightest background colour. In addition, I used darker colours to emphasize the headings, allowing users to quickly identify different content sections and levels of information.


For the font, I chose Shrikhand from Google Fonts. It has a quality that is between handwritten and printed typography, which helps reduce the rigidity of default fonts without being overly messy or making the website feel unprofessional. I think it fits the overall tone of the website very well and also allows me to express my personal style.

For the image materials, I chose resources that I created myself. The profile photo on the About Me page was taken at home, and the suit jacket helps strengthen the professional image of my personal website, and I also used Photoshop to remove the background of my photo. The project photos on the Portfolio page are all taken from actual records of my previous projects. This allows users to gain an initial understanding of each project's topic and characteristics through the images while browsing the page. If users want to learn more about a project, I have also provided relevant links on the page so they can further explore the complete work.

During the design process, accessibility and WCAG 2.0 were also taken into consideration. All images include alt text descriptions, allowing users who use screen readers to understand the content of the images. I also used semantic HTML elements, such as header, navigation, main, and footer, to establish a clear structure and make the website easier and more intuitive to navigate.

From a visual perspective, I also ensured sufficient contrast between the text and background. Dark backgrounds are paired with light-coloured text, while light backgrounds are paired with dark-coloured text, making the website easier to read and browse. All form fields on the Contact page are provided with descriptive labels so that users can understand the purpose of each input field. The embedded video on the same page also includes captions, allowing users who have difficulty accessing audio to understand the content of the video.

Clickable links throughout the website also provide mouse interaction feedback, making interactive elements easier to identify. Headings are differentiated by size and colour according to their hierarchy, creating a clear visual hierarchy and structure throughout the content.
